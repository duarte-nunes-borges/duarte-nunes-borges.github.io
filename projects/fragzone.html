FragZone

A paintball arena built like three Call of Duty maps, an hour from Paris. As CEO of a team of five, I led its business plan and its pitch to investors.

The assignment

Turn a business idea into a business plan solid enough to convince investors to buy shares in the company.

Our role in the exercise
The founding team of a start-up raising money from investors.
Course
Business creation (management & accounting)
When
November to December 2025. Ten days, team of 5

What we had to deliver

  1. Business plan assumptions, with sources
  2. 3-year income statement and free cash flow forecast in Excel
  3. NPV, IRR and payback
  4. Break-even on 3 parameters and 3 scenarios
  5. An investor pitch

My part

CEO of the team: I split the work and kept the deadlines, worked on the financial model and the scenarios, and led the pitch to the jury.

Investor pitch (PDF)

The problem

Paintball in France has no brand.

France has 150 to 200 paintball centres, mostly small and independent, with no national name. Meanwhile, FPS players, groups of friends and companies all look for immersive experiences.

Our answer

Play the maps you know, for real.

An outdoor arena on a 13,000 m² site an hour from Paris, with areas modelled on Nuketown, Shipment and Rust, opening with the yearly Call of Duty release.

How we built it

From the brief to the final pitch, in five steps.

  1. Size the market

    Four segments: groups of friends, families, FPS players and companies, with team building expected to bring 15 to 25% of revenue.

  2. Set the assumptions

    An average price of €60 per player, about 47,000 players and 62% occupancy: €2.79M of revenue a year.

  3. Fund it

    €1.3M needed at launch, 61.5% of it for the licence: a bank loan, founders' money, a tourism loan, public grants, and €300K of equity offered for 20 to 25% of the company.

  4. Forecast it

    Income statement and free cash flow: −€513K in year 1, then €2.4M of cumulative free cash flow by year 5, and a 30% IRR.

  5. Stress-test it

    Break-even on price, clients and revenue, and three scenarios. With 10% fewer clients and a 10% lower price, the IRR falls to −2%: we showed investors exactly where the risk sits.
