Hunted

A 2D horror game concept where the creature remembers where you hid. In two weeks, we built the pitch a publisher would need to sign it.

The assignment

Convince a publisher to sign a new game, with a concept tested on players and a credible plan to launch it.

Our role in the exercise
The marketing team of a young independent studio that has a game idea but no publisher yet.
Course
Marketing & Management of Innovation
When
November 2026. Two weeks, team of 2

What we had to deliver

  1. Market and player research
  2. A game concept, tested with its target players
  3. A business plan: budget, financing, break-even and risks
  4. A go-to-market roadmap, from prototype to launch
  5. A pitch to a jury playing the publisher

My part

Built the whole pitch in a team of two: market research, the player test, the business model, the financing and the deal.

Publisher pitch (PDF)

The problem

Once you know the monster, the fear is gone.

Horror has been the top indie genre on Steam three years in a row, yet story-driven horror games are rarely played twice. In 22 negative Steam reviews of Little Nightmares II, 9 blame scripted fear. Our target player, Lucas, 16 to 25, plays on PC with headphones on and watches horror streams.

Our answer

A creature that never forgets what you did.

Hide in the wardrobe once and it walks past. Next time, it tears the wardrobe open. The creature remembers your hiding spots, your routes and your noises, so no two tries are alike. We placed the concept between LIMBO's world and Granny's threat: a €9.99 PC game with a free demo.

How we built it

From the brief to the final pitch, in five steps.

  1. Read the market

    Steam data, Gamalytic and player reviews to find the gap, then a positioning map and a Blue Ocean grid against eight horror games.

  2. Test the concept with players

    15 players aged 16 to 25. Thirteen wanted to replay it and fourteen found €9.99 fair, but only eight noticed that the creature remembered. So we added claw marks that make the memory visible, and give streamers a moment to clip.

  3. Cost it and finance it

    A €400K budget for a team of four over 16 months, financed by public grants (44%), the founders and a Kickstarter (21%) and the publisher (35%).

  4. Design a deal a publisher can say yes to

    €138K paid in five steps, with a go/no-go gate at each milestone. The publisher takes all sales until it gets 110% of its money back, then 50/50. Break-even at 34,329 copies, tested in three scenarios.

  5. Plan the launch and the risks

    A 16-month roadmap to a launch on Steam, with a free demo at Next Fest, and an FMEA analysis of 12 risks with a fix for each.
