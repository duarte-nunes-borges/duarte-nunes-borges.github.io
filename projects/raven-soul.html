Raven Soul

A one-tap dark-fantasy runner, prototyped by 14 students from the business, art and tech schools. I led its launch plan and co-managed the team.

The assignment

Plan the launch of the game our student studio was making, from its positioning to the KPIs that would prove the launch worked.

Our role in the exercise
The marketing and management team of a 14-person studio, working with tech and art students.

The prototype

What the tech and art students built.

Gameplay from the playable student prototype: one tap to fly Raven through the ruins of Astryx and dodge the barriers. The game was never released.

The problem

One-tap mobile games all look the same.

Hyper-casual games are quick to make and hard to notice. Raven Soul needed a reason to stand out from “one more runner”, and a team of 14 from three schools needed to deliver on time.

Our answer

Give every run a story.

Players guide Raven, the Reaper's messenger, through the ruined city of Astryx to free lost souls. The Ghost Mode power-up becomes part of the story, and the dark-fantasy art sets the game apart from bright hyper-casual games.

The team

14 students, three schools, one deadline.

8 in tech3 in art3 in business, including me

With the two other business students, I ran the project: we split the work into sprints, tracked every task on a Kanban board and held regular check-ins with the art and tech students, so the prototype, its visuals and its launch plan were ready on the same day.

How we built it

From the brief to the final pitch, in five steps.

  1. Position the game

    Gameplay benchmarks (Flappy Dunk, Jetpack Joyride) and art benchmarks (Hollow Knight, Have a Nice Death): one-tap simplicity with a darker tone.

  2. Define the players

    Score chasers aged 14 to 24 and break-time players aged 18 to 30, each with a persona, their needs and what makes them quit.

  3. Set the promise

    One line for the whole game: a full session in one to five minutes, with no tutorial and no paywall.

  4. Choose the channels

    Paid, owned and earned channels, press angles for mobile and indie media, and four creators chosen for their audience, from 25K to 2.9M subscribers.

  5. Time it and measure it

    Six months from pre-launch to post-launch, and KPIs sized for a first indie mobile game: 200 to 500 installs in the first month, day-1 and day-7 retention.
