Gen.G Esports

We audited the online shop of one of the best League of Legends teams in the world, then redesigned it: a brand book and 22 screens in Figma.

The assignment

Help an esports club sell more on its online shop: first find what blocks fans from buying, then redesign the shop.

Our role in the exercise
E-commerce and merchandising consultants, then a digital agency pitching a redesign to the club.

The problem

A top club, with a shop that gets in the way.

Gen.G sells jerseys to fans in Asia, North America and Europe, but its shop made buying harder than it should be: no filters on the catalogue, sizes hidden in a dropdown, product details shown as Korean images on the English site, almost no cross-selling, and a guest checkout that is hard to find.

Our answer

Fix the journey first, then make it look like Gen.G.

We turned each problem into a design decision, wrote the brand book the shop would follow, then designed the mobile and desktop shop in Figma, including what happens when something goes wrong.

How we built it

From the brief to the final pitch, in five steps.

  1. Map how the club makes money

    Partnerships, esports revenue and merchandise. In the catalogue, hardcore fan items average around $65 and lifestyle apparel around $28: two different buyers, two different moments.

  2. Audit the shop, page by page

    Homepage, navigation, product pages and checkout, tested on desktop and mobile over two weeks, and compared with Team Liquid, 100 Thieves, Vitality and T1.

  3. Propose ways to sell more

    A free-shipping bar, buy now pay later, a gift above a basket threshold, and a limited capsule with the fashion brand Studio Nicholson, with its launch plan and follow-up emails.

  4. Write the brand book

    The club's DNA, values and tone of voice, then the rules for the logo, colours and typography.

  5. Redesign the shop in Figma

    17 mobile and 5 desktop screens, and a board that documents the whole journey, action by action.

Before and after

Five problems from the live shop, and how we fixed them.

  • Sizes hidden in a dropdown

    One extra click per product just to check if your size exists, on a shop with no size filter.

  • No filters, confusing pagination

    87 products to scroll through, and pages 6 and 7 hidden behind an arrow.

  • Almost no cross-selling

    Beyond name kits for jerseys, nothing suggests a second item, so the basket stays small.

  • Product details as Korean images

    Sizes, materials and care notes in Korean images: unreadable for international fans and invisible to search engines.

  • A top bar wasted on a hashtag

    The most visible strip of the shop said nothing useful, where competitors put free shipping or a new drop.
